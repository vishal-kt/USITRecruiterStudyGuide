Rate Calculations
Bill rate, pay rate, margin — the math
Must Know
  • Bill Rate = what client pays your company per hourExample: Client pays $80/hr
  • Pay Rate = what you pay the candidate per hourExample: You pay candidate $65/hr
  • Gross Margin = Bill Rate − Pay Rate = $80 − $65 = $15/hrOn 40hrs/week → $600/week margin for the company
  • CTC (Cost to Company) = Pay Rate + Benefits + Employer taxes (~20–25% overhead)W2 with benefits: add ~$8–12/hr overhead on top of pay rate
  • W2 vs C2C equivalence — C2C rates are higher because candidate pays own taxesW2 $60/hr ≈ C2C $70–75/hr rough equivalence
💡 Quick rule: To convert W2 to C2C equivalent, add 20–25%. Client always quotes Bill Rate. Your job: negotiate Pay Rate to keep margin healthy (minimum 15–20% margin).

Boolean Search Mastery
Your most powerful sourcing weapon
Must Know
  • AND — narrows results. Both terms must be presentJava AND "Spring Boot" → must have both
  • OR — broadens results. Either term works(Java OR J2EE OR "Core Java") → any of these
  • NOT — excludes termsNOT (Junior OR Fresher OR Intern OR Trainee OR "Entry Level")
  • Quotes "" — exact phrase match"Machine Learning" → exact phrase, not separate words
  • Parentheses () — grouping for logic(Java OR Python) AND (AWS OR Azure)
Sample Production String:

(Java OR J2EE OR "Core Java") AND ("Spring Boot" OR Microservices OR Hibernate) AND (Developer OR Engineer OR Programmer) NOT (Junior OR Fresher OR Intern OR Trainee OR "Entry Level")
Google X-Ray for LinkedIn:

site:linkedin.com/in "Java Developer" "New York" "Open to work"

Fake Resume Detection
Protect yourself and your client
Must Know
  • Too many skills — "Jack of all, master of none" patternJava + Python + .NET + Salesforce + SAP on same resume = red flag
  • Dates don't add up — overlapping jobs, gaps, impossible timelines
  • No specific project details — only vague role descriptions with buzzwords
  • Copy-paste job descriptions as experience — same language as JD, no metrics
  • Ask technical questions specific to their stated years of experience7-year Java dev cannot struggle to explain OOP, Spring context, or threading
  • Verify LinkedIn profile matches resume — photo, connections, endorsements, activity
  • Check at least one reference — call, don't just email
  • Inconsistent grammar/formatting across sections — copy-paste from different sources

Job Description Analysis
Break down any JD in 5 minutes
Must Know
  • Identify Primary Skills — deal-breakers, listed first, labeled "Required"
  • Identify Secondary Skills — nice-to-have, labeled "Preferred" or "Plus"
  • Note Years of experience — be realistic, ±2 years is usually fine
  • Confirm Location — Remote? Hybrid? Onsite? Which city? Which state?
  • Check Visa restrictions — "Authorized to work without sponsorship" means no H1B
  • Understand the Domain/Industry — Finance, Healthcare, Retail changes the experience needed
  • Calculate if bill rate is realistic for the skill set and location
  • Check Duration — Contract? Contract-to-hire? Direct hire?

Candidate Call Script
How to open, screen, and close on a call
Must Know
  • Opening — first 15 seconds"Hi [Name], this is [Your name] from [Company]. I found your profile on Dice — do you have 5 minutes? I have an exciting Java role in Texas."
  • Work auth check"What is your current work authorization? Are you on H1B? Do you need sponsorship?"
  • Location + availability"Where are you located? This role is [location] — are you open to that? When can you start?"
  • Technical fit check"Tell me about your Spring Boot experience — what version? What was the team size? What were you building?"
  • Rate checkAsk for expected rate first. Never reveal client budget first. "What is your current rate or expected rate for this type of role?"
  • Commitment close"If I submit your profile and you get an interview, will you attend? Are you actively interviewing elsewhere?"
  • RTR — Right to Represent"Can I have your permission to submit your profile to [Client name] for this role?" — always verbal + written
⚠️ Never submit without RTR. It protects both you and the candidate from duplicate submissions and legal disputes.

🛡️ Compliance & Legal Basics
📄
Compliance Checklist
Every recruiter must know these cold
Must Know
  • RTR — Right to RepresentWritten consent from candidate before submitting to any client. Never skip this.
  • I-9 VerificationEmployer must verify identity + work authorization for every new employee before Day 1 of work.
  • E-VerifyFederal electronic system to confirm I-9 eligibility. Mandatory for federal contractors.
  • Background CheckCriminal, employment history, education verification — done post-offer but pre-start.
  • NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)Candidate may need to sign before seeing client project details or SOW.
  • Non-Compete / Non-SolicitationAsk: "Do you have any non-compete with current/previous employer?" It can block placement.
  • MSA (Master Service Agreement)Contract between your company and client/vendor — defines terms, payment, liability.
  • PO (Purchase Order)Client issues PO before work starts — no PO = no payment guarantee. Always chase the PO.
  • Drug TestSome clients (defense, healthcare) require pre-employment drug testing. Disclose to candidate early.

⭐ Senior Recruiter — Advanced Skill Zones
🤝
Account Management
Own client relationships, not just reqs
Advanced
  • Understand client's org structure — hiring manager, HR, procurement
  • Know client's preferred visa types, blacklisted vendors, interview style
  • Build rapport beyond just submitting resumes — check in weekly without reqs
  • Track client's project pipeline — anticipate upcoming hiring needs 4–8 weeks ahead
  • Handle escalations — candidate no-show, withdrawal, rate disputes — professionally
  • Conduct Quarterly Business Reviews (QBR) — present fill rates, placements, value delivered
  • Know client's budget cycle — when they approve new headcount
💬
Advanced Rate Negotiation
Protect margin while closing candidates
Advanced
  • Never be the first to quote — ask candidate's expected rate first
  • Anchor technique: "Our range for this role is $X to $Y — where do you fall?"
  • Handle "I need more" — pivot to benefits, remote flexibility, long-term opportunity
  • Counter-offer handling — ask what it would take before candidate accepts counter
  • Understand blended rates for mixed teams and managed services deals
  • Know when to walk away — if margin drops below viability, let the req go
💡 Senior tip: Always leave $3–5 buffer between your floor and what you quote. Negotiation always happens. If you quote your floor, you lose margin every time.
📦
Bench Sales & Proactive Marketing
Don't wait for reqs — market your bench
Advanced
  • Maintain a hotlist of available consultants: tech stack + location + visa + availability date
  • Market bench candidates proactively to clients/vendors before a req is open
  • Craft compelling marketing emails: "Available: 8yr Java + Microservices, GC, remote-ready"
  • Network with prime vendors to get listed as approved sub-vendor
  • Track candidate availability dates — reach out 2–3 weeks before project end
  • Keep consultants warm even while placed — relationship continuity = repeat business
👥
Team & Delivery Management
Leading a recruitment pod
Advanced
  • Allocate reqs based on recruiter skill strengths — Java-savvy recruiter gets Java reqs
  • Daily standup — pipeline health, blockers, submission counts per recruiter
  • Train junior recruiters — boolean, screening calls, compliance, ATS
  • Set team KPIs and track weekly — submissions, interviews, closures per recruiter
  • Handle difficult situations — team conflict, low performance, client escalations
  • Report to leadership — weekly delivery reports, pipeline forecasting

Top Interview Questions for Recruiters
What hiring managers will ask you
Must Know
  • "Walk me through your end-to-end recruitment process"Mention: JD analysis → sourcing → boolean → screening → RTR → submission → follow-up
  • "How do you identify a fake or padded resume?"Cover: date gaps, skill overload, vague project descriptions, technical verification call
  • "What is the difference between W2, C2C, and 1099?"Be ready to explain tax responsibility, rate equivalence, and use cases for each
  • "How many submissions can you make per day? What are your KPIs?"Junior: 5–8/day. Know your interview ratio and closure rate.
  • "Write a Boolean string for a DevOps Engineer with Kubernetes experience"Practice live — they may actually ask you to write it on the spot
  • "How do you handle a candidate who backs out after accepting an offer?"Have a contingency plan — pipeline backup candidates, communication strategy
  • "What is RTR and why is it important?"Right to Represent — explain why it prevents legal disputes and duplicate submissions

What Makes a Top Recruiter
The real differentiators in US IT staffing
Advanced
  • Speed — Top recruiters respond to candidates within 30 minutes of a req coming inThe staffing game is won in the first hour
  • Quality screening — Fewer, better submissions that actually convert to interviews
  • Relationship depth — Candidates who trust you will call you first when they're availableThis is your unfair advantage over competitors
  • Tech fluency — Knowing enough to have a real conversation with a Java developer
  • Compliance discipline — Never shortcuts on RTR, I-9, background checksOne compliance mistake can cost you a client
  • Communication clarity — Clear, confident US English on calls and in emails
  • Pipeline mindset — Always building 3 months ahead, not just today's req
